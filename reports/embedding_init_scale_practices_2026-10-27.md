# Embedding inits span six orders; nobody swept scale

Across word embeddings, transformer token tables, recommenders, CTR, knowledge graphs, static and temporal graph learning, and metric learning, the expected L2 norm of a freshly initialized embedding **row** spans roughly **6.5e-5 to 11.3 at d=128** — six orders of magnitude — and the field has never published a one-variable sweep of that quantity at fixed learning rate in any of those subfields. The conventions do not converge because they are not solving the same problem: the count-based and production lineages (word2vec, GloVe, fastText, LINE, DLRM, TorchRec, Xavier-on-a-table) start rows essentially **at the origin**, with scale shrinking as `1/d` or `1/sqrt(N)`; a second family deliberately pins the row norm to **Θ(1)** independent of width (Flax `Embed`, fairseq's pre-multiplier table, GPT-NeoX `small_init` at 0.6325, SGNN-HN at 0.5774); and the HuggingFace lineage fixes a width-blind per-coordinate σ=0.02, so its row norm *grows* with width (0.226 at d=128, 1.28 at d=4096). Against that map, a row norm of **1.0 is not an extreme — it is the modal "norm-controlled" convention**, coinciding almost exactly with Flax's default, fairseq's table, and TransE's post-renormalization sphere; a row norm of **1e-3 is below every published convention except hyperbolic embedding**, where it originates. The one mechanism in the literature that *derives* an init scale rather than picking a constant is RotatE's `(gamma + epsilon)/d`, which ties the init to the score margin so the initial logit lands in the sigmoid's high-gradient region. And the single most load-bearing structural finding, reached independently in NLP, KGE and recsys, is that **init scale matters in proportion to how little renormalization sits downstream of the lookup** — which is why a system whose only scale-sensitive path is a final distance with one learned temperature and no weight decay should expect large, dataset-dependent swings exactly like the +0.0319 / −0.0894 MRR split measured here.

Four facts fall out of this table and they are the whole placement answer. First, **row norm 1.0 is ordinary**: it is Flax's default, fairseq's table, TransE's sphere, JODIE's initial state, and within a factor of 1.7 of SGNN-HN, GPT-NeoX and LightGCN. Second, **row norm 1e-3 is extraordinary outside hyperbolic geometry** — the only published tables that start that small are Nickel & Kiela's `poincare-embeddings` repo and HGCN, both of which pair near-origin init with a burn-in phase ([Nickel & Kiela 2017](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1705.08039): learning rate `η/10` for 10 epochs, "because embeddings are initialized close to the origin"). Third, and a correction worth making explicitly: **Nickel & Kiela's "1e-3" is a per-coordinate uniform bound, not a row norm.** At d=128 it yields a row norm of 6.5e-3, so a row norm of 1e-3 is roughly **6.5× smaller than the paper's init** and about 1.5× larger than the repo's actual `1e-4` default — and the repo's Lorentz override is `1e-5`, a 100× disagreement with the paper that persists unexplained in the code. Anyone inheriting "1e-3 from Nickel & Kiela" has inherited one of three mutually inconsistent constants, in the wrong units.

Fourth, the two families **disagree on the sign of the width and size dependence**, and nobody reconciles them. PyTorch's `nn.Embedding` default is `N(0,1)`, unchanged since v1.0.0 ([torch/nn/modules/sparse.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/pytorch/pytorch/main/torch/nn/modules/sparse.py)), which is ~48× larger per coordinate than `nn.Linear`'s default at the same width and is why essentially every NLP codebase overrides it. Xavier applied to an `(N, d)` table is dominated by `N`, not `d`: PyTorch takes `fan_in = N`, so OGB's canonical featureless baseline ([ogbl-ddi gnn.py](https://github.com/snap-stanford/ogb/blob/master/examples/linkproppred/ddi/gnn.py)) silently makes init scale a function of graph size — row norm 0.337 at N=4267, d=256, but 0.0021 at N=1.1e8, a 160× change from nothing but a bigger graph. The production rule is the same family: DLRM and TorchRec both default to `U(±sqrt(1/num_embeddings))` ([dlrm_s_pytorch.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/facebookresearch/dlrm/main/dlrm_s_pytorch.py), [torchrec/modules/embedding_configs.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/pytorch/torchrec/main/torchrec/modules/embedding_configs.py)), so **the bigger the vocabulary the smaller the starting radius** — the opposite of a capacity intuition, and with no published justification from Meta or Google. Over this system's 9k–995k entity range, those rules would prescribe 0.0066 to 0.17, i.e. **6× to 170× above the 1e-3 arm and 6× to 150× below the 1.0 arm**.

## Distance-based scorers are the only family that legislates scale

TransE is the founding statement of the exact degeneracy at issue here. Algorithm 1 initializes both tables `uniform(−6/sqrt(k), 6/sqrt(k))` citing Glorot & Bengio, and then, **at the top of every minibatch iteration, hard-assigns every entity vector to unit norm** — `e ← e/‖e‖`, an equality on the sphere, not a projection onto the ball, and applied to entities only, with "no regularization or norm constraints given to the label embeddings" ([TransE, NIPS 2013](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2013/file/1cecc7a77928ca8133fa24680a88d2f9-Paper.pdf)). The stated rationale is verbatim the degeneracy: the constraint "prevents the training process to trivially minimize L by artificially increasing entity embeddings norms", and the paper credits the same device to three predecessors. Read the init alongside it and the renormalization is not cosmetic — `U(±6/sqrt(k))` has `E‖x‖ = 6/sqrt(3) = 3.464` at every `k`, so **line 5 shrinks every entity by a factor of ~3.46 on the first iteration** (with an extraction caveat: the PDF loses the radical glyph, and if the intended formula is Glorot's `sqrt(6)/sqrt(k)` the invariant is `sqrt(2) = 1.414` instead; the literature almost universally transcribes it as `6/sqrt(k)`).

The sequel matters more. In the largest hyperparameter study of the field — Ruffinelli, Broscheit & Gemulla, ICLR 2020, which made the init distribution *and* its scale first-class search dimensions over `[1e-5, 1.0]` and exposed TransE's entity/relation normalization as two searchable booleans — **the best TransE configuration on FB15k-237 turned the paper's own entity normalization OFF**, with "Embedding normalization (TransE): Entity No, Relation No", L2 regularization instead, Adagrad at lr 0.041, dim 512 ([Ruffinelli et al., Table 6](https://madoc.bib.uni-mannheim.de/54954/1/you_can_teach_an_old_dog_new_tricks_on_training_knowledge_graph_embeddings.pdf)). The same study quantifies init sensitivity per model: the MRR drop for the best configuration *not* using the winning initializer is **−3.7 on FB15k-237 and −5.1 on WNRR for TransE**, against −0.2 to −1.5 for DistMult, ComplEx, RESCAL and ConvE ([Table 3](https://madoc.bib.uni-mannheim.de/54954/1/you_can_teach_an_old_dog_new_tricks_on_training_knowledge_graph_embeddings.pdf)). That ordering is the single most transferable number in the whole survey: **the distance-based model is 2.5–25× more init-sensitive than its dot-product siblings**, and it is the one whose init is entangled with its score scale. (Citation hygiene: arXiv **2004.14447 is not this paper** — that id is a plasma-physics article; the readable copy is the Mannheim repository one above.) The flip side is that the winning *scales* in that search are large — `Unif[−0.85, 0.85]` or `Normal σ=0.806`, i.e. row norms of 5.6 to 18.2 at d=128–512, **30–100× above a Xavier draw on the same table** — so for a dot-product scorer with an L2 penalty the search picks big init, while for a fixed-margin distance scorer the hand-tuned codebases pick tiny init. The discriminating variable is whether the score scale is pinned by a margin.

Which brings us to the one principled alternative to picking a constant. RotatE initializes both tables `U(±(gamma + epsilon)/d)` with `epsilon = 2.0` fixed and `gamma` the per-dataset score margin from {3…30} ([codes/model.py](https://github.com/DeepGraphLearning/KnowledgeGraphEmbedding/blob/master/codes/model.py), [RotatE §4.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1902.10197)). **The mechanism is logit normalization, not variance preservation.** For an L1 distance summed over `d` coordinates of differences of `U(−a, a)` variables, `E[d_r] = Θ(d·a) = Θ(gamma + epsilon)`: the `d` in the denominator makes the *initial score* dimension-free, which is exactly the property a fixed additive margin needs and which "σ = constant" does not have. Simulating the verified init gives initial `d_r ≈ (1.1–1.4)·gamma` on every real benchmark — 27.1 at gamma=24, 11.5 at gamma=9, 8.3 at gamma=6 — so the loss's logit `gamma − d_r` starts within a factor of ~1.4 of zero, in the high-gradient region of the sigmoid. The `epsilon = 2.0` is a floor keeping the init from collapsing as gamma goes to zero; its one failure case is Countries (gamma=0.1), where the floor dominates and the initial distance is ~22×gamma. **Neither the paper nor the code states this rationale** — the formula appears without comment — so the interpretation is the researchers' reconstruction, not an author claim. The idiom nonetheless spread verbatim: byte-identical in HAKE, in DGL-KE's `KEModel` (`EMB_INIT_EPS = 2.0`), and as an opt-in path in OpenKE's TransE ([KGE-HAKE](https://github.com/MIRALab-USTC/KGE-HAKE/blob/master/codes/models.py), [dgl-ke](https://github.com/awslabs/dgl-ke/blob/master/python/dglke/models/general_models.py), [OpenKE](https://github.com/thunlp/OpenKE/blob/OpenKE-PyTorch/openke/module/model/TransE.py)). Translated to a `temp · −dist` scorer with a *learned* temperature, the analogue is to choose the init so the initial distance spread times the initial temperature puts the ranking logits where the loss has gradient — a check that is computable at init and that no amount of constant-picking performs.

The rest of the KGE family splits three ways, and PyKEEN's vocabulary is the cleanest available for the distinction: an *initializer* runs once, a *normalizer* runs every forward pass and is differentiable, a *constrainer* runs after every update without gradients, and a *regularizer* is a penalty in the loss ([pykeen/nn/representation.py](https://github.com/pykeen/pykeen/blob/master/src/pykeen/nn/representation.py)). On that scheme TransE's 2013 mechanic is a **constrainer**, and the common paraphrase "TransE regularizes the embeddings" — used even by the LibKGE paper — is a category error. PyKEEN's TransE reproduces the 2013 asymmetry exactly (`entity_constrainer = functional.normalize`, `relation_constrainer = None`); PairRE revives the hard unit norm, "For entity embedding, the L2-norm is set to be 1" ([PairRE](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2011.03798)); RotatE inverts the assignment, constraining *relations* to unit modulus by reparameterizing them as phases and leaving the entity radius free "for representation capacity", with no regularizer at all; pRotatE and TorusE fix the entity moduli and are RotatE's own ablation that the radius *does* carry information. Meanwhile **LibKGE's default is `normalize.p: -1`, i.e. no normalization**, and the same is true of DGL-KE, OpenKE and every PyKEEN model but TransE. So inside the family that invented the constraint, the constraint is off by default.

## The strongest baselines normalize at score time and leave the table alone

The two methods that currently beat this system on its own benchmark start their tables at almost the same radius, from unrelated code lineages, and both control magnitude **where the score is computed** rather than in the stored parameter. CRAFT learns a free `nn.Embedding(n+1, d, padding_idx=0)` initialized `N(0, 0.02²)` — the BERT constant, default `initializer_range=0.02` and `embedding_size=172`, giving **row norm 0.262** — and then applies one of three `nn.LayerNorm` modules to every node embedding before it reaches the cross-attention or the scorer ([CRAFT models/CRAFT.py](https://github.com/luyi256/CRAFT/blob/master/models/CRAFT.py)). SGNN-HN initializes *every* parameter `U(±1/sqrt(d))` with `stdv = 1.0/np.sqrt(embedding_size)` — **row norm 0.5774 at every d, the only dimension-free rule in the survey** — and then hard-projects to the unit sphere at score time, `x / sqrt(sum(x², -1))`, applied to both the session representation and the candidate item embeddings ([SGNNHN.py](https://github.com/luyi256/CRAFT/blob/master/models/SGNNHN.py)). So the bar sits at **0.26–0.58 stored row norm with normalization downstream**, which is between this system's two arms and an order of magnitude from the 1e-3 one. A side observation from the same read, worth checking before trusting any padding analysis of CRAFT: `self.apply(self._init_weights)` runs *after* `nn.Embedding`'s own `reset_parameters`, and unconditionally renormals the whole table including row 0, so the `padding_idx` row is a random vector of norm ≈0.26, not zero — `padding_idx` only zeroes the gradient.

This is the general pattern, not a quirk of two repos. Across NLP, **almost nobody constrains the table's own radius, while almost everybody controls the post-lookup scale**: a `grep` for `max_norm` returns zero hits in fairseq's transformer, nanoGPT, HF `modeling_utils.py`, HF BERT, DLRM, PyG's `Node2Vec`/`MetaPath2Vec`, DGL's `NodeEmbedding`, TGN and every DyGLib model. PyTorch's own `nn.Embedding(max_norm=…)` exists and renormalizes per row, but its documented side effect explains the avoidance — it "will modify the `weight` tensor in-place" during the forward pass, which breaks the autograd/optimizer contract, interacts badly with weight tying, and is incompatible with sharded tables ([sparse.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/pytorch/pytorch/main/torch/nn/modules/sparse.py)). What the field uses instead is LayerNorm or RMSNorm immediately after the lookup, the `sqrt(d_model)` multiplier as an explicit norm-*setting* device (fairseq's `embed_scale`, SASRec's `scale=True` at a factor of 7.07 at d=50), or an explicit `F.normalize` in the forward graph. FixNorm is the rare case that constrains the table itself — `g·w/‖w‖` with the underlying table at `U(±0.01)` — and reports **+0.61 BLEU average** over PreNorm+LayerNorm across five low-resource pairs, with the caveat that the row changes normalization and init simultaneously ([Nguyen & Salazar 2019](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1910.05895)). JODIE is the most aggressive controller found anywhere: two learned vectors total, `F.normalize(torch.rand(d))`, shared by every node, with dynamic states re-projected to the unit sphere at every update ([jodie.py](https://github.com/srijankr/jodie/blob/master/jodie.py)).

The metric-learning literature is where this becomes a complete, measured design. The construction is uniform: L2-normalize the embedding (and usually the class weight), delete the bias, multiply the cosine by a scalar `s`. **`s = 64` is the de facto standard** — CosFace sets it empirically and says explicitly it is "more favorable to fix the scaling parameter s instead of adaptively learning" ([CosFace](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1801.09414)); ArcFace inherits it ([ArcFace](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1801.07698)); AdaFace and MagFace follow; `pytorch-metric-learning` ships `scale=64` for both. Two papers derive a **lower bound on `s` growing like log(number of classes)**: L2-constrained softmax's `α_low = log(p(C−2)/(1−p))` ([Ranjan et al.](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1703.09507)) and CosFace's `s ≥ ((C−1)/C)·ln((C−1)P_W/(1−P_W))`; instantiating either at C=10,575 and p=0.9 gives **≈11.46** (the researchers' arithmetic from the formulas — neither paper prints a number), so production `s` sits ~5× above its own bound. NormFace is routinely cited for this bound and **could not be read** in this survey — ar5iv returns metadata only and the PDF would not extract — so its proposition must not be attributed from the CosFace formula.

The measured sensitivities are large and they cut both ways. Ranjan's α sweep on IJB-A 1:1 TAR@FAR=1e-4 runs **0.257 at α=8, 0.620 at α=12, 0.744 at α=24, 0.727 at α=32**, with plain softmax at 0.553 — a scale too small destroys training outright, and LFW at α=1 collapses to 86.37%. On the upper side, ArcFace's one clean `s`-ablation shows margin-free Norm-Softmax at **s=64 scoring 98.56 / 89.79 / 88.72 against s=20's 99.20 / 94.61 / 92.65** (LFW / CFP-FP / AgeDB) — up to **+4.82 points for the smaller scale** — and the paper states it "searched the best scale parameter s=20 for Norm-Softmax". So `s=64` is the right value *given a margin*, not in general. The same shape appears in contrastive learning: SimCLR's Table 5 gives ImageNet linear-eval top-1 of **64.4% at τ=0.1 against 59.7% at τ=0.05 and 58.0% at τ=1 with ℓ2 normalization, and ~57% without normalization at any τ in {10, 100}** ([SimCLR](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2002.05709)) — about **7 points**, the strongest published evidence that a free embedding scale is not merely redundant with a temperature but actively worse. CLIP is the outlier in *learning* the scale, `logit_scale = log(1/0.07)` starting at 14.3, "clipped to prevent scaling the logits by more than 100 which we found necessary to prevent training instability" ([CLIP](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2103.00020)); the one paper that measured fixed-vs-learned found learning strictly worse or equal (0.698 vs 0.744; 0.832 vs 0.832). Ring loss is the soft version — a convex penalty `(‖f(x)‖₂ − R)²` toward a **learned** target radius, argued for on the grounds that the hard equality constraint is a non-convex feasible set SGD never actually satisfies — and it reports CFP frontal-vs-profile VR@1e-3 rising **55.86 → 87.43** over plain softmax and being far less brittle across λ than the hard L2 constraint, whose own α is reported as exploding at 50 and failing to converge at 30 ([Ring loss](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1803.00130)).

On the degeneracy itself, the algebra is uncontroversial and explicitly written down — temperature enters the loss *only* through the rescaled logits `Z = βz` — but the best source **declines to call the two redundant**, because β also sets the effective learning rate `η̃ = ηβ²` and the nonlinear timescale `τ_nl ∝ β/η̃` ([Temperature check](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/2010.07344)). To separate the knobs at all the authors had to engineer a correlated initialization with `‖Z⁰‖_F = β·sqrt(1+c)·‖z⁰‖_F`; having done so, they measured CIFAR-10 accuracy "strongly sensitive to β but largely insensitive to ‖Z⁰‖_F" once the learning rate is rescaled, with empirically optimal `η* ∝ 1/β`, an optimal β "often far from 1" (1e-2 without batchnorm, 10 with, 0.3 on ImageNet ResNet-50), and **+0.86 ImageNet top-1 from temperature alone** (76.51 → 77.37). They also report that "final training-set logit norms tend toward a similar value regardless of initial β and ‖Z⁰‖_F" — the network partly absorbs a temperature change by moving its own scale. **The precise, supportable claim is therefore: scale and temperature are exactly redundant in the loss value and the ranking, and not redundant in the optimization** — and the whole softmax-temperature literature is inner-product based, with no source deriving the homogeneity argument for a distance score.

## Too-small and too-big are both documented failure modes, and the notes reconcile them

The LLM-stability literature says unambiguously that **too-small embedding scale is the failure mode**. Spike No More gives two conditions for stable pretraining — small sub-layers and "a large shortcut", meaning "the standard deviation of each embedding close to 1" — and implements the second as "Scaled Embed", multiplying embeddings by `sqrt(d)` ([Spike No More](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.16903v4)). The measured payoff is not a tuning delta: at 1.7B, WikiText perplexity falls 22.58 → **21.29** and LAMBADA 15.22 → **12.53**; at 13B, vanilla init **diverged** at lr 3e-4 while Scaled Embed trained there to WikiText 14.47 / LAMBADA 5.97, beating the best vanilla run (lr 1e-4) by 0.65 ppl and 0.5–2 points on six downstream benchmarks. Xavier, violating both conditions, produced the most frequent loss spikes. **Going above std 1 does not help** — std 1, 5 and 50 all prevented spikes but above 1 degraded performance, though the paper gives loss curves only and no numeric perplexities for 5 and 50. OLMo 2 reaches the same mechanism from the opposite direction: "standard weight decay shrank token embeddings too far, leaving them very small. Small embeddings can produce large gradients in early layers", so they **disabled weight decay for embeddings**, after which "embedding norms settle in a healthy region" ([OLMo 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.00656v3)). Two independent large-scale projects converging on "embeddings too small relative to the sub-layer path", one fixing it at init and one during training, is the strongest convergent evidence in the whole survey. (OLMo 2 is internally inconsistent on whether its 0.02 init is normal or truncated normal, and on whether its z-loss is 1e-4 or 1e-5; its measured init ablation dropped the gradient-norm spike score from **0.40 to 0.03**, and its embedding-decay trade was spike score **0.16 with decay vs 0.092 without**.)

Two NLP ablations point the other way. Nguyen & Salazar's en→vi dev BLEU has **Xavier+PostNorm failing outright (fail / fail / 5.76 at 4k/8k/16k warmup) against SmallInit+PostNorm at 28.17 / 28.20 / 28.62** — but with PreNorm, Xavier recovers to 28.52 and SmallInit is level, so the effect size is "decides whether training happens" or "worth ≈0 to −0.3 BLEU" depending on one architectural choice ([Nguyen & Salazar](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1910.05895)). And a June 2026 single-lab study parameterizing every weight matrix as `σ = d_in^−γ` finds **γ=1 beating γ=0.5 on validation loss and on 8/8 downstream benchmarks at 1.5B (average 24.98 vs 22.47, +2.51)**, with γ>1 degrading ([arXiv 2606.17945](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.17945)). That result is **not an embedding-only ablation** and must not be reported as one: the sweep applies to all matrices, and its interest here is theoretical — at γ=1 the embedding norm is Θ(d^−1/2), the word2vec regime, while HF's 0.02 is Θ(d^1/2) and fairseq/Flax/NeoX are Θ(1).

**Do not average these.** The reconciliation the notes offer is structural and is independently confirmed three times: **init scale is consequential in proportion to how little renormalization sits downstream of the lookup.** Nguyen & Salazar's effect is enormous under PostNorm and ~zero under PreNorm. The γ result interacts strongly with RMSNorm ε (worth ~0.038 at γ=1 but ~0.001 at γ=0.5) and with gated attention. Spike No More's mechanism is explicitly the *ratio* of embedding scale to residual-branch scale. The HF 0.02 constant has survived from BERT-2018 to LLaMA-3 across a 10× growth in `d` — a 3.3× drift in init norm that nobody adjusted — precisely because a fully pre-normed architecture renormalizes it away. KGE says the same thing in its own vocabulary (init matters 3.7–5.1 MRR for the distance scorer, 0.2–1.5 for the bilinear ones), and so does recsys: Rendle et al. 2020, whose entire thesis is that hyperparameters dominate architecture, used σ=0.1 and report in so many words that they "tested some variation of the standard deviation but did not see much effect" ([Rendle et al.](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2005.09683)) — measured at d ≤ 192 on ML-1M and Pinterest, **with λ tuned to 0.005–0.01 and 256 epochs**, i.e. with a real L2 penalty and enough time for the table to reach a scale set by the loss/penalty balance regardless of where it started. That is not evidence that init scale is harmless in a short-run, unregularized, million-entity, distance-based regime. A system whose pooler features are all scale-free ratios and whose only scale-sensitive path is a final distance has **nothing downstream of the lookup to renormalize anything** — it is the maximally init-sensitive configuration in this taxonomy, and a ±0.03–0.09 MRR swing between row norms 1e-3 and 1.0 is what the structural reading predicts.

## Adam turns a rescaling into a learning-rate change, and no weight decay removes the equilibrium

Two invariances are routinely conflated. Adam **is** approximately invariant to rescaling the *gradient* — Kingma & Ba's stated design property, which holds only under ε = 0 and, by a 2026 analysis, only to first order unless β1 = β2 ([Unit Scaling App. E.2.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2303.11257), [arXiv 2601.21739](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.21739v1)). Adam is **not** equivariant to rescaling the *weights*, because its per-step displacement is ≈η per coordinate once the moment ratio saturates, regardless of `‖w‖`. **No paper found states this as a formal proposition** — two searches returned only the gradient-rescaling result — so the following is derivable-but-uncited arithmetic: the relative displacement per step is ≈`η·sqrt(d)/‖w‖`, so doubling a table's init scale **halves its effective relative learning rate** while leaving the initial function unchanged if a learned output scale absorbs the difference. For this system's numbers — η=1e-3, d=128 — a saturated Adam step has norm ≈0.0113, which is **~11× the entire row length at init norm 1e-3 and ~1.1% of it at init norm 1.0**: a four-orders-of-magnitude change in effective relative step, from an init change that the forward pass can absorb into one learned scalar. LARS/LAMB's trust ratio `‖w‖/‖g‖` is exactly the correction, making the step proportional to the weight norm ([LAMB](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1904.00962)); Adam without it is the non-equivariant case.

Rotational Equilibrium supplies the quantitative anchor: under AdamW the equilibrium norm is `‖ω‖ ≈ sqrt(ηC/2λ)` with `C` the dimension, **independent of gradient magnitude**, while plain Adam with ℓ2 gives a cube-root expression that *does* depend on gradient magnitude, "so rotation can be imbalanced across layers" — measured at **~30× variation in rotation across layers, roughly a 1000× range in effective learning rate** ([Rotational Equilibrium](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.17212v3)). Two consequences bite here. First, **with no weight decay there is no equilibrium at all**: the table's scale is set entirely by its initialization plus accumulated one-way drift, and the λ=0 recurrence implies the expected squared norm grows by about `η²C` per step with nothing to balance it (the researchers' inference from the recurrence, not stated by the paper). The paper also notes explicitly that **scale-sensitive layers deviate from the equilibrium theory "because a radial gradient component changes the effective weight decay"**, naming ResNet-50's final FC layer; embedding layers are not mentioned anywhere in it.

Second, this is exactly why **post-step norm projection is a disguised learning-rate change unless the trained radius was going to sit there anyway.** The one careful head-to-head of a pure post-step rotational constraint against AdamW finds a tie on CIFAR-10 (92.4 vs 92.2) and OpenWebText GPT-2 (3.18 vs 3.18), a slight win on Wikitext GPT-2-124M (**19.0 vs 19.6 ppl**), and one catastrophic failure: **IWSLT2014 Transformer-S at 20.0 BLEU zero-shot against the baseline's 34.6, recovering to 34.7 only after re-tuning** — precisely because that baseline "was trained with effectively zero weight decay and never reached equilibrium". Projecting a table to a fixed radius is safe only when the unconstrained run was heading there; otherwise it changes the effective LR and needs its own LR retune, which is what "few-shot" meant in those results. Reparameterization is a different object from projection: weight normalization exposes the radius as its own parameter with its own Adam state ([Salimans & Kingma](https://arxiv.org/abs/1602.07868), numeric results not obtained), while nGPT normalizes everything onto the hypersphere and reports **4–20× fewer training steps**, with a follow-up attributing part of that to added per-step compute ([nGPT](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.01131), [anTransformer](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.22014v2)).

The theory the reader might hope to borrow does not transfer, and the reason is sharp. For **degree-0 homogeneous** (scale-invariant) parameters, three things are proven: the gradient is orthogonal to the weight, the squared norm therefore grows monotonically as `‖w_{t+1}‖² = ‖w_t‖² + η²‖∇‖²`, and the effective step on the sphere is `η/‖w‖²` ([Arora, Li & Lyu](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1812.03981), [SMD](https://arxiv.org/html/2006.08419v4)). **A `temp · −dist` score is degree-1 homogeneous in the table, so Euler's identity gives `⟨E, ∇_E f⟩ = f ≠ 0`: the radial gradient component is nonzero and the orthogonality lemma is unavailable.** The radius is actively driven by the loss rather than accumulating noise. What survives is a *joint* invariance: `(E → cE, τ → τ/c)` leaves the function exactly unchanged, so the loss surface has a flat direction along which `(‖E‖, τ)` can drift with no change in loss, while Adam's trajectory still depends on where on that orbit training starts. **No source analyses a degree-1 homogeneous parameter with a learned output scale**; the closest published acknowledgements are the ResNet-50 final-FC caveat above and a GPT-2-without-weight-standardization observation. Finally, muP's prescription is the one point on which muP, Cerebras-GPT and DeepMind's 2024 sweep agree: an embedding table has finite fan_in, so it is an "input weight" whose **init variance and Adam learning rate are both width-independent constants to be tuned directly, not derived** ([Tensor Programs V](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2203.03466)); Cerebras names exactly three transferable hyperparameters, one of which is the embedding output multiplier ([Cerebras-GPT](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2304.03208)); and the 26.8B sweep found standard parameterization with a width-independent embedding LR **beating muP** (loss 2.572 vs 2.599) ([Scaling Exponents](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.05872v1)). For a fixed-width model this reduces to: the embedding table's init scale and learning rate are free constants that must be tuned separately from everything else — which is itself the claim that embedding init scale is not a no-op.

## Norm growth is asserted everywhere and plotted nowhere

Growth is documented, with the clearest number from outside embeddings: in T5 pretraining, **parameter norms grow roughly as `sqrt(training steps)`**, and weight decay "only prevents growth when its coefficient is set too large" — within the standard range, norms grow in the face of it ([Merrill et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2010.09697)). In a CNN with BatchNorm and no weight decay, norm growth cut the effective learning rate **~10× over the first 50 epochs** ([Three Mechanisms](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1810.12281)). The remedies with measured effect in large transformers target the *logit*, not the parameter: QK-layernorm "enables stable training across three orders of magnitude of learning rate variation" and let a 1.2B model train at LR 0.3, with the measured thresholds that **max attention logits above 1e4 always diverged and enforcing ~1e3 begins to hurt loss**; z-loss at coefficient 1e-4 resolved output-logit divergence, which "appeared with no weight decay at every scale tested" ([Small-scale proxies](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.14322v2)). That paper is widely cited for init scale and **contains no init-scale sweep at all** — it studies logit growth and output-layer scale; attributing an init-scale ablation to it would be wrong.

For embedding tables specifically the measurements are end-of-training correlations, never curves. In Euclidean dot-product recommenders, **Pearson correlation between item-embedding L2 norm and item popularity runs 0.47–0.84** across three datasets under both BPR and sampled softmax, and removing the norm channel *at inference alone* improves tail recall by double-digit percentages ([TTEN](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2308.11288)). The face-recognition analogue is quantified once: **Pearson 0.5235 between feature norm and an image-quality proxy at the final epoch**, higher than the correlation of the ground-truth probability and visible early in training ([AdaFace](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2204.00964)) — and notably AdaFace, which believes the norm is informative, still uses only its *batch-relative* position via an EMA-standardized `(‖z‖ − μ_z)/(σ_z/h)`, h=0.33, which is the strongest practical signal that absolute norm scale is not trustworthy across training. In graph learning the claim is figures-only: "high-degree nodes … tend to have embeddings with larger norms", with no coefficients, and a self-loop correction worth **+11.22 to +18.19 Hits@20 on Cora/CiteSeer** but only +1.55 to +3.87 on CoraFull/PubMed ([Norm Augmented GAE](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.05868v1)).

**The sign is geometry-dependent, which matters for any hyperbolic reading of radius growth.** Euclidean GAE work treats norm∝degree as a bias to correct by *raising* low-degree norms; hyperbolic work treats radius∝generality as the representation itself, with Nickel & Kiela stating that "the hierarchical organization of the space is solely determined by the distance of points to the origin" and making **no claim linking norm to degree** ([Nickel & Kiela 2017](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1705.08039)). One hyperbolic recommender reports a **negative** correlation, −0.360 between item norm and purchases — search-summary level only, and plausibly an artefact of a parameterization that places items near the origin by construction, so it should be read directly before use ([arXiv 2409.02599](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2409.02599)). A separate industrial study reports content-embedding norms growing sharply with view count under *batched* updates and weakly under real-time updates ([arXiv 2312.15265](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2312.15265), numbers unextractable), which suggests norm inflation is partly an optimizer/accumulation artefact rather than a signal about the entity — directly relevant to a radius climbing at a near-constant rate per epoch. Two last constants worth knowing: geoopt and HGCN both clamp the float32 Poincaré coordinate norm at `1 − 4e-3`, i.e. **hyperbolic radius 6.2126**, while Nickel & Kiela's repo clamps at `1 − 1e-5`, radius **12.206** — a float32 *precision* choice silently acting as a modelling cap.

## Four holes in the literature, independently identified

**First, there is no embedding-table-only init-scale sweep at fixed learning rate, anywhere.** All four subfield surveys reached this conclusion separately: no such experiment in NLP (every located ablation varies the global init scale or couples init to a normalization change), none in recsys (not Rendle 2020, not Dacrema 2019, not LightGCN, which runs no init ablation at all despite its code departing from its paper's stated Xavier to `N(0, 0.1)` on an unquantified comment), none in graph learning (two independently phrased searches returned only init-*strategy* papers), and none in KGE — LibKGE searched init scale over `[1e-5, 1.0]` jointly with everything else and reports only the winner plus the drop for the best config not using that *distribution*. The largest measured init effect found in graph learning, Refined-GAE's **94.43 / 13.84 / 2.13 Hits@20 on ogbl-ddi** for orthogonal / random / all-ones learnable tables, **cannot be cited for scale**: the arms differ in rank and inter-node correlation as well as magnitude, the "random" arm is `U(0, 1)` from PyTorch's defaults and not the `[−1, 1]` the paper's text describes, the orthogonal arm has row norm `sqrt(d/N) ≈ 0.49` rather than the "approximately unit norm" its own Section 4.1 analysis assumes, and ogbl-collab barely moves across all four arms (65.16–66.11) ([Refined-GAE](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.03845v4), [train_wo_feat.py](https://github.com/GraphPKU/Refined-GAE/blob/main/train_wo_feat.py)).

**Second, no per-epoch embedding-norm trajectory is published anywhere.** No KGE paper reports mean or max entity norm as a function of epoch; no recsys source reports norm curves, only end-of-training correlations; no graph or temporal-graph paper reports a trace of node-embedding norm; OLMo 2 shows embedding norms in a figure with no numeric values; and no study measures norm growth specifically in a node or item table. The practice of logging `r_mean`/`r_max` per epoch has **no published precedent to compare against**.

**Third, TransE's renormalization has never been ablated as one variable.** No measurement exists of a distance-based model with and without the constraint at fixed everything else, and no one has quantified how much MRR the norm-inflation shortcut costs if left unconstrained. LibKGE's tunable normalization flag is the nearest proxy and it is one cell of a joint search, not a controlled contrast — and its WNRR TransE column is not reported for that row.

**Fourth, the degree-1-homogeneous-plus-learned-temperature case has no treatment.** The scale-invariance theory that would supply the norm-growth and effective-LR results is proved for degree-0 homogeneity, and **by Euler's theorem a degree-1 score has a nonzero radial gradient component, so it does not transfer**. The softmax-temperature literature that handles the scale/temperature redundancy is entirely inner-product based; no paper derives the homogeneity argument for a distance score, nobody states the redundancy as a theorem (the field treats it as an obvious reparameterization and moves straight to which knob to pin), and the highest-value unread source on the cosine case — Kobayashi, "Temperature in Cosine-based Softmax Loss", ICCV 2025 — returned HTTP 403. Two adjacent holes belong here: **no paper constrains the mean norm `E[‖x‖]` directly** (Ring loss is per-sample toward a shared learned radius; AdaFace standardizes by the batch mean without constraining it), and **no measured comparison exists of projection onto a ball versus onto a sphere**, which is exactly the geoopt-clamp question. Finally, two widely repeated claims should be treated as folklore until checked: that **GPT-3 and LLaMA exclude embeddings from AdamW weight decay** — neither paper's optimizer section nor any official code confirmed it, and LLaMA reports wd 0.1 without stating exclusions — and that the HF `initializer_range: 0.02` in a released LLaMA or OPT config says anything about what Meta trained with (HF's `_init_weights` runs only when a model is built from config, never when loading pretrained weights; OPT's rumoured 0.006 is unverified and should not be quoted).

## Conclusion

The field's apparent disagreement about embedding init scale dissolves into a single question that is rarely asked out loud: how many renormalizations sit between the lookup and the loss? Where there are several — a LayerNorm on the embedding sum, a pre-norm residual stream, an `F.normalize` at score time, a `sqrt(d_model)` multiplier that overwrites the table's own scale — the init constant is nearly free, which is why σ=0.02 survived a 10× growth in model width unexamined and why Rendle could not find an effect. Where there are none, the init scale *is* the model's scale, and the only two mechanisms that have ever been designed rather than inherited both act on that fact: TransE deletes the degree of freedom by projecting to the sphere every minibatch, and RotatE sizes the init so the initial score lands at the margin. A system with scale-free pooler features, a single learned temperature, a final distance and no weight decay has removed every renormalization and every restoring force at once — so it sits at the far end of this axis, where a one-variable init change is simultaneously a geometry change, a four-orders-of-magnitude change in Adam's relative step, and the only thing anchoring the table's scale for the whole run.

Three practical implications follow that the literature supports but never states together. A row norm of 1.0 is the field's modal norm-controlled choice and needs no defence, while a row norm of 1e-3 is a hyperbolic convention that arrives bundled with a burn-in phase and, read as a row norm rather than a coordinate bound, is smaller than the paper it came from; if the near-origin arm is kept, the burn-in should be kept with it. A dataset-dependent sign flip between the two arms is the expected outcome rather than an anomaly, because every measured init effect in this survey is conditional — on normalization placement (Nguyen & Salazar: fail versus 28.2 with PostNorm, ±0.3 BLEU with PreNorm), on whether a margin is present (ArcFace: s=64 wins with a margin and loses by 4.8 points without), on model family (LibKGE: 3.7–5.1 MRR for TransE, 0.2–1.5 for the bilinear models), and on dataset (Refined-GAE: a 92-point spread on ogbl-ddi, one point on ogbl-collab). And the untried move with the strongest published rationale is not another constant but RotatE's: compute, at init, where the initial score distribution lands relative to the loss's high-gradient region, and set the scale from that — the one place in this literature where an init was derived from the objective instead of copied, and the only alternative on offer to a sweep the field has never run.
