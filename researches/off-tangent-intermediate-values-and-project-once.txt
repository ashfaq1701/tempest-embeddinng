================================================================================
Off-tangent intermediate values + project-once: is it valid on the sphere?
================================================================================

Context
-------
In NeighborhoodProjection (link_property_prediction/model.py, the multi-head
displaced-tangent attention head) the per-token VALUES and the per-head outputs
mu_h are general R^d vectors that are NOT on the tangent space at E[u]: the
learned displacement (W_v.g_p + Enc(stable)) and the head-mix W_o both leave the
(d-1)-dim tangent subspace {v : <v, E[u]> = 0}. The design projects the COMBINED
aggregate onto the tangent space ONCE, at the very end (mu -= <mu,E[u]>.E[u]),
then applies exp_{E[u]}.

The question researched: is "compute freely off the tangent space, project once
at the end, then exp" mathematically valid and accepted practice, or do people
insist every intermediate value stays on the tangent plane?


================================================================================
VERDICT: Valid and standard — and project-once is the BETTER choice here
================================================================================

The pattern is mathematically valid and matches accepted, foundational practice
in Riemannian / manifold deep learning. It is not considered wrong. For our
pipeline specifically -- where every op before the final projection is LINEAR
(the displacement map, the attention-weighted sum, the head-mix W_o) and the
sphere's tangent projection P_x(v) = v - <v,x>x = (I - x x^T) v is itself LINEAR
-- the pattern is cleaner than the general case, and projecting ONCE at the end
is strictly more information-preserving than projecting at every step.


1. The pattern IS the definition of a retraction (foundational support)
-----------------------------------------------------------------------
Riemannian optimization is built on "take a step that leaves the manifold, then
map back." This is the framework, not a shortcut.

- Boumal, "An Introduction to Optimization on Smooth Manifolds" (2023),
  Example 3.49 / Eq. 3.32 gives our sphere case verbatim: to move from x along v
  while staying on the sphere, take the step in R^d then project back:
  R_x(v) = (x + v) / ||x + v||. The step x+v is OFF the sphere; normalization
  returns it. (Def. 3.47; Sec. 5.12 metric-projection retraction, Eq. 5.45.)

- Absil & Malick, "Projection-like Retractions on Matrix Manifolds" (SIAM J.
  Optim. 22(1), 2012): thesis is that "taking a tangent step in the embedding
  Euclidean space followed by a projection onto the submanifold is a retraction."
  Sec. 2.2: "x + eta_x in general does not belong to M ... The concept of
  retraction was introduced to provide a framework for these approximations to
  the exponential update." Leaving the manifold for intermediate computation and
  mapping back is the STATED PURPOSE of the framework.

- Absil, Mahony & Sepulchre, "Optimization Algorithms on Matrix Manifolds"
  (2008), Def. 4.1.1, with local-rigidity DR_x(0) = id (retraction agrees with
  exp to first order).

- geoopt implements exactly this: Sphere.retr(x,u) = projx(x+u) = (x+u)/||x+u||;
  RiemannianAdam / RiemannianSGD compute the update in the tangent/ambient space
  and retract back.


2. Tangent-space neural nets do exactly this (with one nuance)
--------------------------------------------------------------
Dominant hyperbolic/spherical NN template: Log -> ordinary Euclidean
linear/attention -> Exp.

- Ganea et al., Hyperbolic Neural Networks (NeurIPS 2018): a map's Moebius
  version is f^{\otimes c}(x) := exp_0^c(f(log_0^c(x))) (Def. 3.2); Moebius
  matmul is exp_0(W log_0(x)).

- Chami et al., HGCN (NeurIPS 2019), Eq. 6: W \otimes^K x^H := exp_o^K(W
  log_o^K(x^H)) -- "project hyperbolic points to the tangent space, then project
  back using the exponential map."

- Gulcehre et al., Hyperbolic Attention Networks (ICLR 2019): aggregates via the
  Klein/Einstein midpoint (weighted coordinate combination), then projects back.

NUANCE (the adversarial catch): in the Poincare ball, the tangent space at the
ORIGIN is all of R^n, so a generic W never "leaves" anything -- the orthogonality
issue is vacuous there. On the SPHERE and the Lorentz hyperboloid, the tangent
space at a general base point IS a proper subspace {v : <v,x> = 0}, and there a
generic dense W genuinely leaves it. Fully Hyperbolic Neural Networks (Chen et
al., ACL 2022) is the primary critique: existing tangent-space linear layers use
a STRUCTURED block-diagonal matrix that zeroes the base coordinate to stay
orthogonal, and they prove this is "a relaxation of the Lorentz rotation and does
not include the boost" -- i.e. STRICTLY LESS EXPRESSIVE than a general manifold
map. Their alternative accepts a generic W and re-derives the base coordinate to
land back on the manifold.

Upshot for us: letting W (and W_o) leave the tangent subspace and re-projecting
once is the MORE EXPRESSIVE choice, not the impoverished one. Papers that "stay
on the tangent subspace" do so by RESTRICTING W -- exactly the restriction the
fully-hyperbolic line criticizes as a limitation.


3. "Weighted sum in ambient space, then project" is a named aggregation
-----------------------------------------------------------------------
Our attention-weighted aggregation followed by a single projection is the
EXTRINSIC MEAN.

- Bhattacharya & Patrangenaru (Annals of Statistics 31(1), 2003): the extrinsic
  mean embeds points, takes the ordinary ambient arithmetic mean (which lies OFF
  the manifold), then projects to the closest manifold point. On S^{d-1} the
  closed form is normalize(sum_i w_i x_i). Preferred because it is FASTER than
  the intrinsic Karcher/Frechet mean, which has no closed form for >2 points and
  needs iterative Log/average/Exp.

- Spherical k-means (Dhillon & Modha, Machine Learning 42, 2001): centroids are
  normalize(sum x_i); the mean of unit vectors is not a unit vector until
  projected.

- HGCN aggregates by weighted Euclidean sum in the tangent space then Exp,
  stating the reason: "the Frechet mean ... has no closed form solution." The
  accepted one-step approximation.

All have exactly our property: the intermediate weighted sum lives off the
manifold and is returned by a single normalization/projection.


4. The subtlety that matters: project-once != project-every-step (once is better)
---------------------------------------------------------------------------------
First-principles linear algebra (follows cleanly; no single ML paper states it as
a theorem):

- P_x(v) = (I - x x^T) v is LINEAR, idempotent, symmetric. It distributes over
  weighted sums: P_x(sum_i w_i v_i) = sum_i w_i P_x(v_i).

- For a chain of linear layers, project-once = P_x A_k ... A_1 v0, while
  project-every-step = P_x A_k P_x ... P_x A_1 v0. These are equal for all inputs
  IFF every intermediate map keeps its output in the tangent subspace --
  otherwise they DIFFER, because intermediate projection DISCARDS the off-tangent
  (span-x) component that a LATER linear layer could rotate back into the tangent
  space.

- Therefore project-once retains STRICTLY MORE information. This is an argument IN
  FAVOR of our design. Order matters (P_x . A != A . P_x for generic A), so
  "project once at the very end" is deliberate, not an accident.

- The clean equivalence to "operate on the manifold throughout" holds only to
  FIRST ORDER, because the true intrinsic op uses the NONLINEAR exp/log, of which
  P_x is the linearization at x. Our pipeline has exactly one nonlinearity before
  projection (the softmax attention weights -- scalars) plus the final exp_x, so
  there is no per-step nonlinear distortion for the projection to compound. The
  "tangent space is only a local approximation" critique (Dai et al., H2H-GCN,
  CVPR 2021) targets networks that REPEATEDLY round-trip through exp/log; it does
  not bite a single-projection, single-exp design like ours.


5. Gradient / numerical behavior of the final projection: benign
----------------------------------------------------------------
Backprop through the final projection is the SAFE part. v -> v - <v,x>x has
constant Jacobian I - x x^T (for fixed base point x) -- no singularity, nothing
to clamp. The documented numerical hazards in this literature (catastrophic NaNs,
vanishing gradients) come from the NONLINEAR maps -- exp/log/arcosh, normalization
v/||v|| as ||v||->0, boundary effects -- not from the linear tangent projection
(Mishne et al., "The Numerical Stability of Hyperbolic Representation Learning,"
ICML 2023: Float64 radius bounds ~19-38; Poincare gradients shrink like delta^2
near the boundary; geoopt clamps accordingly). Keep guards on exp_x / Log_x, not
on the projection. (Our expmap already uses torch.sinc + clamp_min norms; the
radial clamp keeps ||mu|| < pi.)


================================================================================
Which convention is more common, and why
================================================================================
"Compute in a Euclidean/tangent parametrization and project (or exp) back" is BY
FAR the more common convention -- the entire tangent-space school of
hyperbolic/spherical NNs, the retraction framework in optimization, and
extrinsic-mean aggregation. It wins on simplicity, speed, numerical stability,
and reuse of ordinary Euclidean layers.

The competing "stay intrinsic / never leave the manifold" school (Fully
Hyperbolic / Lorentz-direct nets, H2H-GCN, exact Frechet-mean aggregation) exists
and argues two real points: (a) tangent-space linear maps at a FIXED base point
are less expressive (no Lorentz boost) and non-equivariant to base-point choice;
(b) repeated exp/log round-trips distort global geometry. BOTH critiques are about
REPEATED round-trips and RESTRICTED linear maps -- the opposite of what we do. Our
design (unrestricted linear ops off the tangent space, ONE projection, ONE exp)
sidesteps both.

Two honest live disagreements in the literature (for the caveats section):
- Whether the exp/log round-trip HURTS stability (Chen et al. 2021) or a
  single-exp Euclidean parametrization HELPS it (Mishne et al. 2023) is contested.
- Whether backprop through a final exp_0 causes gradient vanishing (Guo et al.
  2022) is explicitly rebutted by Mishne et al. (Remark 4.3): the exp_0 Jacobian
  "reduce[s] the power of delta from 2 to 1 and thus help[s] alleviate the
  gradient vanishing problem."


================================================================================
Bottom line for NeighborhoodProjection
================================================================================
Valid and standard. Keep the current design (unconstrained W_v / W_o, single
final projection). It is effectively an extrinsic/projected aggregation with a
learned ambient transform -- MORE expressive than the tangent-preserving
structured maps of classic tangent-space nets, and STRICTLY MORE
information-preserving than projecting at every step. The "tangent-faithful"
variant (project per-value + scalar/diagonal W_o) would be LESS expressive and
LESS information-preserving; only adopt it if base-point equivariance is a hard
requirement. Keep numerical guards on exp_x / Log_x, not on the projection.
